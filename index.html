Gravitational Potential Gradient & Field Relation (E = -dV/dr)

Gravitational field intensity is the negative spatial derivative of gravitational potential with distance (E = -dV/dr = -∇V). The potential gradient vector ∇V points uphill toward higher potential (outward), whereas the gravitational field E always acts downhill toward decreasing potential (inward to center).

Gravitational Mechanics of Solid Spheres & Hollow Shells

Inside a uniform solid sphere, gravitational field increases linearly with distance (E ∝ r) and vanishes at the center (E = 0). Inside a hollow shell, field intensity is zero (E = 0) everywhere, while potential remains constant and equal to the surface value (V = -GM/R).

HSC Physics: Gravitational Formulas Summary

1. Potential Gradient Relation (বিভব নতিমাত্রা সম্পর্ক)

Vector form: E = -∇V = - (∂V/∂r) r̂
1D Radial form: E = - dV/dr
Interpretation: The negative sign denotes that the gravitational field acts in the direction of greatest decrease of gravitational potential (i.e. toward the mass).

2. Thin Hollow Spherical Shell (পাতলা ফাঁপা গোলক)

  • Inside (r < R): E = 0, V = -GM/R (Constant)
  • Surface (r = R): E = GM/R², V = -GM/R
  • Outside (r > R): E = GM/r², V = -GM/r

3. Uniform Solid Sphere (সুষম নিরেট গোলক)

  • Inside (r ≤ R): E = (GM/R³) r, V = -(GM/2R³)(3R² - r²)
  • Center (r = 0): E = 0, V = -1.5 (GM/R) = 1.5 V_surface
  • Outside (r > R): E = GM/r², V = -GM/r

4. Superposition in Concentric Shells (সমকেন্দ্রিক গোলকদ্বয়)

For inner shell (M₁, R₁) and outer shell (M₂, R₂):
In cavity (r < R₁): E = 0, V = -GM₁/R₁ - GM₂/R₂
Between shells (R₁ ≤ r < R₂): E = GM₁/r², V = -GM₁/r - GM₂/R₂
Outside (r ≥ R₂): E = G(M₁+M₂)/r², V = -G(M₁+M₂)/r
